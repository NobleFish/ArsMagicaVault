A leader can usually only have a number of men under his command equal
to his Leadership score. However, larger groups can be handled through
having the leader include subordinates in his command, who each command
their own men. For example, a Dux with a Leadership of 4 commands 3
soldiers and an Optio. The Optio has a Leadership of 3, and he therefore
commands another 3 soldiers. The effective size of the trained group is
8. There are disadvantages behind indirect command. Firstly, any
Leadership rolls made by the overall leader to affect the entire group
(such as those under Advanced Group Combat, later) have Ease Factors 3
higher than normal. Secondly, issuing commands that affect the whole
group takes up the leader's whole action, although the subordinates can
relay the commands as a "free" action.
