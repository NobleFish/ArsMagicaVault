By Paul Tevis

The Peak of Storms is never visible, as the top of the mountain is
always hidden by clouds. In winter, a cold wind blows down its slopes,
threatening to freeze an unwary traveler solid. During the spring and
summer, thunderstorms form around the mountain. On Midsummer’s Day, a
great storm cloud takes shape over the peak, and at noon a tremendous
thunderclap splits the air. It is said that any child born that day in
the valley below will have the power to speak with the four winds.

Climbing the mountain is treacherous. In winter, it is almost
impossible, as snow covers the upper slopes and the frigid winds blow
with great intensity. In the summer it is easier, but not without
danger. The more serious threat comes from the fogs that hide the peak.
A climber may become lost and stumble into one of the ravines that dot
the slopes. During a summer storm, the ascent is almost as dangerous as
in the winter.

If the peak is reached, the climber finds an ancient and charred tree.
Each Midsummer’s Day this tree is struck with a bolt of lightning. If an
agate pendant is hung from the tree on that morning, when the bolt
strikes it the pendant will be transformed into Auram vis.
