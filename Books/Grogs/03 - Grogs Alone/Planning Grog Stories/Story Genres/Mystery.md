Mystery adventures rely less on combat and more on quick-wittedness and
sharp eyes. These stories can be more fun without magi, who can often
easily solve mysteries with magic. Be careful not to allow a mystery
adventure to devolve into a set of Perception + Awareness rolls to spot
clues.

Examples include:

- A disease is afflicting the grogs, but it has no apparent cause and
  the physicians are baffled. Clues point toward a grog who died
  recently, but why would he have had a grudge against the covenant ?-
  The oblates at a local monastery are going missing. They've been
  trying to cover it up, but eventually a boy connected to one of the
  covenfolk becomes a victim. Where are they going, and is the monastery
  involved?
- The agent of the covenant's merchant contact is found hanged in a
  warehouse. He is discovered to have been involved in the sale of
  stolen relics. But who murdered him — the thieves, a rival, or a
  disgruntled buyer?
