Apply these quality and size multipliers to prices where appropriate.
For example, a handsome riding horse (Excellent) costs considerably more
than a filthy nag (Shoddy), a Standard house might be made of timber
whereas a Superior dwelling is built of stone (although note that a
Standard castle is built of stone), a diamond counts as an Excellent
gem, a goat counts as a Large sheep, and so on. Note that both quality
and size multipliers can apply, for example, something that is both Huge
and of Excellent quality has a total multiplier of 25.

**Land**

One virgate of arable land, or two virgates of wild land. . . . . . . .
. . . . . . . . 10 pounds

One virgate (or yardland) is equal to about 30 acres (although the exact
size varies by region), which is sufficient land to support one peasant
family reasonably comfortably. A square mile is about 20 virgates.
Purchase prices of land vary widely; a typical price is between 20 and
50 times its gross annual income.
