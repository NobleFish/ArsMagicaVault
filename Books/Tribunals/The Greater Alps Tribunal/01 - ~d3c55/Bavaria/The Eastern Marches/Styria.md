Styria is a broad swathe of foothills that stretch from the Austrian
Alps down to the plains of Eastern Europe. Historically it is a border,
a defensive line held by the Romans, and later Christians, against the
steppe peoples who emerged from central Asia. Although there have been
garrisons here since Roman times, Styria, along with Austria, was
founded to defend the flank of the Frankish empire.

During the period of relative peace in the 11th century, churches
sprouted throughout this country much as castles sprouted elsewhere.
This was part of the effort toward consolidation of the territory made
by the ruling family, who died out in 1192. The new rulers, the
Babenbergs, have continued in much the same vein.
