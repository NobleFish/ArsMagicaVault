Mount Pilatus takes its name from the unquiet spirit of Pontius Pilate.
Those who climb the mountain anger him, and he retaliates with terrible
weather, which damages the crops in the Lucerne region and makes the
lake unnavigable for a few days. Even priests are forbidden to climb
Pilatus, because few think that they can exorcise a spirit being justly
punished by the Lord. His body lies incorrupt in an icy pool and he
descends from the mountain once a year to wash his hands in the lake,
which is said to then turn to blood. Hermetic magi once attempted to
harvest vis from this ancient ghost. The following tribunal both made
the act illegal and sent its commiserations to the sodales of the magi
crushed in the avalanche that spoiled the attempt.
