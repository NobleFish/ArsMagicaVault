The Alpine style of wrestling is a strength-based sport. Although
agility is useful for attempting throws, this style of wrestling
emphasizes power. A bout begins with two men matched by height if there
are many contestants — standing on a circle of wood shavings. They shake
hands and take up the starting stance. Each is bent over at the waist,
resting their weight equally on both legs. Each grips the other on the
belt and on the hem of short, sturdy trousers used in this sport. A
referee starts the match, and then the men try to throw each other. The
first whose back hits the wood shavings loses. Friendly matches last for
five minutes. Those involving money continue until someone falls. As an
alternative to hitting the ground, a wrestler also loses if his opponent
holds him in a bridge. The bridge is a lock hold where the loser is
face-upward and is bent over the upper leg of his opponent, which is
thrust into the small of the loser's back.
