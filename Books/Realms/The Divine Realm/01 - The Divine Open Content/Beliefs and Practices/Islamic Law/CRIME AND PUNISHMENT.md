The *shari'a* divides crimes into these categories:

a\) Deliberate killing or wounding, giving the victim or his family the
right to exact vengeance, under the control of the authorities. In some
cases vengeance can be replaced by a ransom.\
b) Accidental killing or wounding, dealt with by financial settlement.\
c) Five major crimes, dealt with using canonical penalties (**hudud**
\[**h**u-DOOD\]):

| Crime | Penalty |
|----|----|
| Theft | Cutting off the right hand |
| Brigandage | Death, if a murder is involved, otherwise as theft |
| Adultery | Stoning or flogging |
| False accusation of adultery | Flogging |
| Use of alcohol | Flogging |

Some other crimes also incur severe penalties. For example, apostasy is
punished by death. Less serious offenses are punished with a verbal
reprimand. Islamic law often requires a number of witnesses to a crime,
making it difficult to impose penalties without adequate proof and
moderating the more severe penalties somewhat.
