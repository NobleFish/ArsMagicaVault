Magic can also be employed to save money. For example, the fertility of
the covenant's lands may be improved, or the magi might magically create
tools or lab equipment, instead of purchasing them. A magical item might
reduce the Consumables costs, for example, an oven which requires no
fuel. Alternatively, it might save on labor, for example, a broom which
sweeps by itself. In the former case, one or more pounds is saved
directly, in the latter case, you can achieve the same results with
fewer covenfolk (thus saving money indirectly). The minimum required
numbers of servants and teamsters may thus be reduced. Magical items can
also perform the work of laborers and craftsmen, and may be treated as
such for the purposes of cost-saving. As a very simple rule of thumb,
each magnitude of effect in such items can save one pound per year. Each
casting of a Ritual spell may save approximately five pounds per
magnitude, although this depends on the nature of the effect (and the
discretion of the storyguide). Although Ritual spells may make dramatic
improvements, such as creating a barnful of grain, they are somewhat
rarely employed, since most magi value the necessary vis at more than
five pounds per pawn. At the storyguide's discretion, non-ritual Rego
craft spells, if cast repeatedly over an extended period, may save money
as per magic items.
