The legends of pagan peoples were generally recorded by members of the
clergy following the conversion of their country. In Ireland, for
example, stories were gathered in the fifth and sixth centuries, in
Wales in the eighth and ninth centuries, and in Scandinavia in the
eleventh and twelfth centuries (Snorri Sturluson, the great compiler of
the Norse myths, is still alive in Iceland in 1220). As folk tales,
these stories of gods and pagan heroes live much longer in the popular
memory, although some may have been cleaned of paganism to pacify the
Church.
