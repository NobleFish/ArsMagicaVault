Authors who write about fairy tales can become very strident about which
version of a story precedes the others, and about the division between
genuine folk tales and literary retellings. Storyguides should not be
too concerned about telling the “genuine” version of a faerie tale. Two
stories that share the same motifs are not the same story: color,
atmosphere, and detail matter and need to be tailored to the players.
For game purposes, the versions of Red Riding Hood where she is saved by
a woodcutter, eaten by the wolf, and seduces the wolf are all good
material. Which version of a story is the best depends on the troupe,
and the saga.
