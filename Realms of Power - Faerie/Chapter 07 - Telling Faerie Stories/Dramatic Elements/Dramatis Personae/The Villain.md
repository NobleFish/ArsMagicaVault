The villain opposes the hero several times during the story, cumulating
with a fight or other form of struggle, and/or a pursuit. There is often
more than one villain in a long story — one who tricks the hero, one who
sets tasks for the hero, one who fights the hero, one who pursues the
hero, and so forth.

Examples: Thrym (in the Theft of Thor’s Hammer); Hera (in the Sorrows
and Labors of Heracles); Kostchei the Undying (in Maria Morevna).
