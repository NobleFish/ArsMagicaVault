The Muslim holy war doctrine has roots in the life of Muhammad but was
not officially formulated until the 8th century. The Arabic word,
**jihad** (ji-HEHD), means a struggle, and encompasses both the struggle
one wages against one's own inner sinfulness (the "greater *jihad*") and
the external struggle one wages against evil (the "lesser *jihad*"),
which in the medieval period mostly takes the form of warfare against
non-Muslims. For most Muslims the *jihad* should be led by the political
authorities of the Muslim world, but individual Muslims are also under
obligation to ensure its success, particularly when it is waged
defensively. Numerous rules, drawn from the *hadith*, regulate the
conduct of the *jihad*, including rules regarding who may fight and
against whom one may fight, prohibitions on the killing of women,
children, and monks, and the destruction of trees and crops, treatment
of prisoners and distribution of plunder.
